About

As a geographer specializing in large-scale ecological analysis, I am interested in the drivers that push species toward extinction or invasiveness. I use a trait-based approach to underpin these patterns and situate my research within a socio-ecological framework, studying both the mechanisms of extinction and invasiveness and their functional consequences, and examining how human societies shape biodiversity for cultural, social, and economic purposes.

My PhD focuses on the impact of human uses on the functional diversity of all vertebrates. I investigate whether certain trait combinations are more targeted by humans and could lead to functional erosion of diversity. I place this work within a biogeographic framework to disentangle regional variation in the functional impact of human activities on vertebrates. Finally, I investigate the impact of non-native species on the functional diversity of native faunas, and which human activities and/or trait combinations facilitate the invasiveness of species

Beyond the field and the data, I am deeply committed to an inclusive, collaborative, committed and international scientific practice. When I am not on the field, teaching or coding in R, you will find me hiking with my dog and camping in the mountains.
